# Vision and product principles

## A practical memory for physical work

Organizations often document *what* should happen, while their most experienced
people know *how* to make it happen. That difference matters. A manual may say
“align the component,” but an expert notices the resistance, the angle, and the
small visual cue that confirms the component is seated correctly.

Vision CoDev's long-term vision is to help organizations preserve this practical
knowledge as a living, reviewable resource and deliver it when a person needs it
most: during the work itself.

## What we are building toward

We imagine a workflow in which:

- experts can demonstrate work naturally instead of authoring a manual from
  scratch;
- the system helps turn the demonstration into structured instructions;
- a responsible reviewer controls what becomes an approved procedure;
- workers can learn with visual and spoken assistance while keeping their
  attention on the task;
- unexpected situations lead to a pause, escalation, or an explicitly approved
  recovery path; and
- organizations can improve procedures using reviewed evidence rather than
  anecdotes alone.

## What makes the approach different

### The recording is evidence, not decoration

An instruction should be connected to the moment that supports it. Reviewers
need to see why a step exists, and workers benefit from examples grounded in an
actual approved demonstration.

### Human approval is part of the system

Machine-generated instructions can be incomplete or wrong. Vision CoDev treats
automated analysis as a proposal. A person with appropriate authority decides
what is approved, what needs correction, and when a procedure should be
withdrawn or replaced.

### Recovery matters as much as detection

Merely announcing that something looks wrong is not enough. Useful guidance
must help the worker understand the issue and either return to a reviewed state
or escalate to the right person.

### Real-world evidence outranks a polished demonstration

A laboratory demo is not proof that a system works across different people,
lighting, equipment, camera positions, and network conditions. Vision CoDev's
validation approach emphasizes representative recordings, physical-device
testing, measurable outcomes, and clearly stated limitations.

## Design principles

1. Keep the expert accountable for the approved process.
2. Preserve a traceable relationship between instruction and evidence.
3. Communicate uncertainty instead of hiding it.
4. Prefer a safe pause or human escalation over improvised guidance.
5. Design for the realities of hands-on work, including interruptions and
   imperfect connectivity.
6. Separate organizations' data and provide controlled access.
7. Measure the complete experience, not only model accuracy.

## What Vision CoDev is not

Vision CoDev is not intended to be an unsupervised authority, a universal
understanding of every physical task, or a substitute for safety engineering.
It is an assistive platform whose usefulness depends on reviewed procedures,
appropriate deployment, representative validation, and responsible human use.
